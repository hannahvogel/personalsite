Hannah Vogel

Hannah Vogel is an archaeologist studying ableism and disability in the ancient world and in the disciplines that interpret it.

Her work brings Critical Disability Studies into conversation with bioarchaeology, palaeopathology, Egyptology, material and mortuary culture, human osteology, and medical imaging. She is a PhD candidate and sessional teacher at Macquarie University, with a parallel commitment to accessible research practice and inclusive higher education.

Hannah joined the Macquarie Theban Tombs Project field seasons in Luxor in 2024 and 2025. She has also worked in public engagement and as Centre Manager and Outreach Coordinator for the Centre for Ancient Cultural Heritage and Environment.

Research

Hannah's current interests include:

  • ableism and Critical Disability Studies;
  • disability, health and wellbeing in the ancient world;
  • bioarchaeology and palaeopathology;
  • Egyptology, mortuary practice and material culture;
  • human osteology, X-ray and computed tomography; and
  • queer theory, gender and embodiment.

Education

Her 2021 Master of Research thesis was titled Contesting Ableist Narratives in Egyptology: A Theoretical Recalibration of ‘Disability’ in Ancient Egypt. She completed a Bachelor of Ancient History at Macquarie University between 2016 and 2018.
