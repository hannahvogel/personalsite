Hannah Vogel

Hannah Vogel is an archaeologist researching ableism and disability in ancient societies and in historical and archaeological scholarship.

Hannah is a disability activist and advocate for accessibility. Her work includes public outreach, education and inclusive teaching. She is a PhD candidate and Sessional Teaching Academic in History and Archaeology in Macquarie University's School of Humanities.

Hannah participated in the Macquarie Theban Tombs field seasons in Luxor, Egypt, in 2024 and 2025. She previously managed the Centre for Ancient Cultural Heritage and Environment and coordinated the Department of History and Archaeology's Studying the Past outreach program.

Research

Her research examines disability, health and wellbeing across ancient societies. It considers how people and communities experienced disability in different periods and places. Her research also addresses identity and belonging, diversity and inclusion, and accessibility in past and present contexts.

Hannah's theoretical interests include historiography, ethics, Critical Disability Studies, queer and gender theory, and embodiment. Her archaeological interests include material culture and mortuary behaviour. Her work in biological anthropology includes paleopathology, human osteology, X-ray imaging and computed tomography.

Education

Hannah completed a Master of Research at Macquarie University in 2021. Her thesis was titled Contesting Ableist Narratives in Egyptology: A Theoretical Recalibration of ‘Disability’ in Ancient Egypt. She completed a Bachelor of Ancient History at Macquarie University in 2018.
